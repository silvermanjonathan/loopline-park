Chunk 1 · TriageE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Three lanes on the board

Every ride breaks the night before opening day — real parks budget the whole morning for it, and so do we. Run your build from yesterday once, exactly as you left it, and put your name in a lane:

RUNNING — booth and coaster both work, start to finish. Head for the Stretch Garage below.
ALMOST — it runs, but something's wrong: a message misprints, the wrong track loads, the ride stalls.
STUCK — it won't start: a traceback before the window even opens.

Predict Whichever lane you're in: before you touch anything, what is the first line you will read, and where is it?
The last line of the traceback if there is one — the week's oldest rule, still undefeated. If there's no traceback (the ALMOST lane's silent bugs), the first thing you read is the dashboard: SPEED 0 means the floor is missing, a STALLED banner means a hill out-talled the lift, a parked train at 4 mph means the position line lost its old self. Being in STUCK this morning is not failure — it's why Expansion Day exists, and why the ribbon isn't until Friday.
Chunk 2 · The clinic rulesE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Nobody's hands on your keyboard

The clinic runs in pairs: one engineer drives, the partner reads over their shoulder — out loud, last line first. Nobody fixes code on someone else's machine, including the teacher. Every fix goes in the two-line debugging log: what broke, what fixed it. The log is collected today — it's your proof that the fixing was yours.

Predict From yesterday's soft launches, three bugs will show up all over the room this morning. Before you tap: name the two most likely crashes, and the one most likely silent failure.
The crashes: a missing : on an if line (SyntaxError — refuses to run at all), and = where == belongs in the mood decision (also SyntaxError — an assignment where a question goes). The silent one: the STALLED banner — a later hill out-talling the lift. That last one isn't a Python bug at all; it's a physics bug, your inspector caught it, and the fix is a smaller number, not different code. If your bug isn't one of these three, the protocol doesn't change: run it, read it, log it, fix one thing, run again.
The Stretch Garage · for the RUNNING lane

Upgrades, tested and priced

One rule before any upgrade: copy your working version into a comment first. Engineers never modify the only working copy. Then pick:

Teach the engine friction

Change the drop line to drop = top - height - position * 0.01 and rerun: the stock ride's top speed dips from 70 to 69 mph. Then build a 99 m Fury-style lift hill and raise the 0.01 until the predicted top speed lands on the real Fury 325's 95 mph — the honest number is up near 0.08. Be ready to say where the missing speed physically went.

The mystery ride

A third booth option where track gets chosen by random.choice() from a list of your track lists — Tuesday's spinner, now spinning whole roller coasters.

The fast-pass lane

A second decision in the booth: fast-pass guests get a different greeting, a shorter message, or their own express track.

Opening-day polish

A grander boarding pass with f-strings, a renamed ride caption, repainted park colors — cosmetic dials, all safe.

Chunk 3 · Prove your own rideE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Grid B — the Ride Log, on your numbers

Yesterday you checked the machine on the stock track. Today you check it on yours. Flip your Ride Log to Grid B: circle five towers on your own track, fill the Height column straight from your list before you run, then catch TIME and SPEED at each as the train crosses. One tower per run; reruns are free; turtle riders leave TIME blank. Grid B is collected today.

Predict — commit before you run Circle the row with your smallest Height. Will it also be your biggest SPEED — and why must that always be true on this engine?
Always. The speed line asks the track exactly one question — how far below the lift hill am I? — so the lowest point is the biggest withdrawal is the biggest speed, on your track, on anyone's track, every time. If your list and the dashboard disagree at any tower, one of them is wrong — and finding out which one is the actual job of an engineer.
Chunk 4 · The upgrade noteE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Version 3 — what changed, and why

Every engineer ships at least one change today: a Garage upgrade, or a track redesign with a goal (Version 3). The rule stands: working version into a comment first — never modify the only working copy. Then, under the Version note on your Ride Log, write today's line: what you changed, and why. One sentence, collected.

Before you write it The weakest upgrade note in the park is "I made it better." Say your note out loud first: does it name the change and the goal? "Raised the third hill to 40 so the middle stops dragging" — that's an engineer talking.

Choose your exit gate

Floor · approaching the standard

Your bug count went down today — your log shows at least one fix you made yourself — and your ride runs start to finish.

Middle · meets the standard

Your build runs, Grid B matches your own list at five towers, and one upgrade shipped with a what-changed-and-why note that names the change and the goal.

Stretch · exceeds the standard

The friction tune lands your 99 m build on Fury 325's real 95 mph, or your booth gains a second decision and survives a guest typing nonsense — and you can explain where the missing speed physically goes, or which line makes the booth unbreakable.
